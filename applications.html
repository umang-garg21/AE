Now

Liquid cold plates for AI chips

The problem
Kilowatt-class accelerators need direct-to-chip liquid cooling, and every new chip generation needs a new plate, designed against tight thermal and pressure limits.
What the engine does
Explores skived, machined and printed copper families, sizes fins and manifolds, and judges each plate on mean case temperature and the hottest point on the chip.
Where it stands
Running end to end with CFD-gated rounds. The current milestone is the first plate that meets a real chip’s limits in self-checked CFD.
Now

Printed heat exchangers

The problem
Additive manufacturing allows cores and headers no machine shop could make, but designing them still takes specialists months of iteration.
What the engine does
Chooses the whole part: architecture, cell count, headers and alloy, then checks pressure drop, effectiveness and wall stress at worst-case tolerance.
Where it stands
Whole-part designs have run live in the governed engine. Compressible header CFD is being qualified.
Next

Thrust chambers and propulsion

The problem
Regeneratively cooled chambers live at the edge of materials: extreme heat flux at the throat, thin printed walls, no margin for guesswork.
What the engine does
The same thermal-fluid core: cooling channels, wall temperature and pressure loss, judged with the same rules and the same ledger.
Where it stands
Next domain pack. Not yet built; the engine’s thermal-fluid core is the foundation.
Horizon

Machines and assemblies

The problem
Real products are systems: parts that must fit, connect and work together under shared constraints.
What the engine does
Component libraries and assemblies, so the engine can reason from a requirement down to every part of a machine.
Where it stands
On the roadmap, after single-part domains are verified.
